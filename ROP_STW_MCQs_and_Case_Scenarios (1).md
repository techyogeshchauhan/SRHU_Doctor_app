# ROP STW MCQs and Case Scenarios


### 1. Which of the following is the appropriate Key Performance Indicator (KPI) for monitoring the quality of ROP prevention and management practices in an SNCU?
- A. Number of babies referred to an ophthalmologist
B. Percentage of eligible preterm/LBW babies receiving at least one ROP eye examination
- C. Total number of babies receiving oxygen therapy
D. Percentage of babies discharged without oxygen requirement

**Answer:** B

### 2. All of the following measures are important for prevention of ROP EXCEPT:
- A. Maintaining appropriate oxygen saturation and avoiding unnecessary oxygen exposure
B. Early and appropriate treatment of neonatal sepsis 
C. Ensuring mothers own milk and appropriate weight gain
D. Liberal blood transfusion for anaemia

**Answer:** D

### 3. Which of the following is the preferred method for arranging ROP screening? ## Case Scenarios


### Case 1

**Scenario:** A preterm 32 weeks, 1.5 kg male baby at 48 hours of life, delivered by LSCS, is receiving CPAP with pressure of 5 cm H2O and FiO2 of 40%.  His SpO₂ is persistently 97-98%. His respiratory rate is 65/minute with minimal chest indrawing, no grunting and no nasal flaring. What is the most appropriate action for managing the respiratory support ?

**Answer:** 
Gradually reduce oxygen (FiO2) while continuing monitoring for respiratory distress and saturation

### Case 2

**Scenario:** An SNCU admitted 20 babies during the month who were eligible for ROP screening. On reviewing the records, it was found that 16 babies received their first ROP screening on time, while 4 babies were screened late. What was the KPI for timely first ROP screening in this SNCU?

**Answer:** 
**Timely first ROP screening = (Number of eligible babies screened on time ÷ Total number of eligible babies) × 100**
**= (16 ÷ 20) × 100**
**= 80%**

### Case 3

**Scenario:** A district SNCU does not have an ROP-trained ophthalmologist available on-site. The medical officer is planning how to ensure timely ROP screening for eligible preterm infants. What should be the most appropriate arrangement for ROP screening?

**Answer:** 
If on-site screening is unavailable, the SNCU should arrange scheduled specialist visits or ensure safe referral to the nearest designated ROP screening center. Tele-screening may also be used where available.

### Case 4

**Scenario:** A 30 wks, 1400 g infant is scheduled for ROP screening. During preparation, the nurse asks what measures should be taken immediately before the examination to improve comfort and safety. What preparation should be done before ROP screening?

**Answer:** 
The infant should have feeds withheld for 1 hour before screening; pupils dilated with phenylephrine 2.5% and tropicamide 0.5–0.8% eye drops; be swaddled; receive proparacaine eye drops; receive EBM/oral 25% dextrose for analgesia; and have temperature and SpO₂ monitored.

### Case 5

**Scenario:** A 28 wk gestation and 900g birth weight baby is diagnosed with aggressive ROP in both eyes and is given intravitreal injection of Ranibizumab in both eyes at 34 weeks PMA. He has been followed up till 10 weeks, and there are no signs of reactivation, but there is persistent avascular retina in zone 2. No further vascular growth is observed. Should the child be exited from the screening program?

**Answer:** Answer.
Babies injected with anti-VEGF drugs like Ranibizumab need longer regular follow-up at least until 65wks PMA, as it can lead to late reactivation of disease or persistent avascular retina, which may need laser treatment.

### Case 6

**Scenario:** A baby born at 32 weeks gestation and 1800 g birth weight was referred to an ophthalmologist for ROP screening after one week of birth. The ophthalmologist did not examine this baby and advised that he needs to be screened at a later date. Why did he advise the same?

**Answer:** 
As per the current ROP screening guidelines, based on gestation age and birth weight criteria,  this baby should undergo the first ROP screening at 4 weeks postnatal age.

### Case 7

**Scenario:** A male preterm infant born at GA 30 weeks, BW 1300 gms, previously admitted to one SNCU due to respiratory distress syndrome and early onset neonatal sepsis, was later transferred to another SNCU closer to his home at 3 weeks of life. He is now brought by his parents at 5 months of age to the ophthalmology department with complaints that the child was not recognising faces or following light. Ocular examination reveals bilateral white reflex, and ultrasonography reveals retinal detachments in both eyes. What is the likely diagnosis and possible reasons for the condition?

**Answer:** 
The baby is likely to have stage 5 ROP. This baby was never screened for ROP and this could be because of failure to identify the infant as “eligible for screening”, lack of a documented  ROP screening plan at the time of back referral and failure to inform parents about the importance of eye exam.

### Case 8

**Scenario:** A 28-week, 1000 gm infant is diagnosed with  bilateral Zone I Aggressive ROP at 35 weeks PMA inside the NICU. What is the likely treatment and what are the checklists at the time of discharge from NICU.

**Answer:** 
Aggressive ROP in zone 1 is treated with Intravitreal Anti-VEGF drugs. Discharge should mention about the ROP diagnosis, date and type of injection given and parents should be informed about the need for regular follow ups for reactivation.
