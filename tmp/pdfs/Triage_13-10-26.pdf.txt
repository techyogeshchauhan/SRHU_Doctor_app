SHOCK
• IV NS 10 mL/kg if volume expansion is indicated: preterm over 30–60 min; term over 15–20 min.
• Consider inotropes early.
SEVERE DEHYDRATION
(Diarrhoea plus any two of lethargy,
very slow skin pinch and sunken eyes)
• Diarrhoeal severe dehydration: IV RL/NS 30 mL/kg in 1 h, then 70 mL/kg over 5 h (WHO Plan C).
• If IV not possible: ORS 20 mL/kg/h for 6 h. Assess 1–2 hourly and titrate fluids.
• Hypernatremic dehydration related to poor feeding/hot environment: manage as per the standard
neonatal protocol.
SEIZURES
(Refer to STW on neonatal seizures)
• Inj. phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IV over 15 min. If seizures persist after 15 min, give a further 10 mg/kg IV
over 10 min.
• If uncontrolled, consider levetiracetam as second-line treatment.
• Correct glucose and calcium if abnormal, concurrently.
SURGICAL
• Cover exposed skin defects with warm saline sterile gauze; maintain hydration.
• CDH: Avoid CPAP/bag-mask ventilation. Intubate if necessary; decompress stomach with an OG/NG tube.
• Suspected TEF/oesophageal atresia: Nil by mouth, IV fluids, head-up position; upper-pouch suction.
• Arrange urgent referral to a surgical centre.
PERSISTENT HR
>200/MIN
• Check ECG for arrhythmia.
• Check and correct hyperthermia; refer for further management.
HYPOTHERMIA
(Refer to STW on thermal care of
newborn)
• Temperature <35.5°C: nurse under a radiant warmer in servo mode.
• Temperature 35.5–36.4°C and baby is well: warm environment, skin-to-skin contact; recheck in 30 min.
• If unwell or temperature fails to improve, nurse under a warmer.
HYPOGLYCEMIA
(Refer to STW on neonatal
hypoglycemia)
• Blood glucose <45 mg/dL and asymptomatic: supervised breastfeeding or EBM.
• Blood glucose <20 mg/dL OR symptomatic: 2 mL/kg 10% dextrose IV, followed by infusion at 6
mg/kg/min.
JAUNDICE
(Refer to STW on neonatal jaundice)
• Serious jaundice (onset <24 h, palms/soles yellow, or signs of acute bilirubin encephalopathy): intensive
phototherapy; consider IV fluids if dehydrated; prepare for exchange transfusion as indicated.
DOs
DON’Ts
• Screen every neonate immediately on arrival for emergency and
priority signs.
• Maintain TABC, assess SpO₂ and blood glucose, and start indicated
treatment promptly. Reassess after each intervention.
• Administer the first dose of antibiotic within 1 hour if there is a high
likelihood of sepsis.
• Do NOT delay emergency treatment for investigations or
documentation.
• Obtain blood culture before the first dose of antibiotics, but do not
delay treatment.
• Do NOT discharge a neonate with persistent emergency or priority
signs; arrange admission or appropriate referral.

• Place under radiant warmer
• Attach temperature probe and pulse oximeter
• Assess for emergency signs using TABC & CD
T: Temperature; A: Airway; B: Breathing; C: Circulation; C: Coma/convulsions; D: Dehydration
Classify into EMERGENCY, PRIORITY or NON-URGENT based on signs given below
EMERGENCY
• Significant hypothermia (<35.5°C)
• Apnea or gasping
• Respiratory distress (retractions, grunt)
• Central cyanosis or SpO₂ <91%
• Shock (cold peripheries, mottled/grey skin, CRT >3 s,
weak pulses, low BP)
• Convulsions/coma
• Severe dehydration
• Persistent tachycardia >200/min
PRIORITY
• Temperature 35.5 to 36.4°C
• Weight <1800 g
• Large for gestation with risk factors (IDM)
• Tachypnea without retractions or grunt
• Severe jaundice (onset <24 h, palm or sole staining)
• Severe pallor
• Bleeding
• Major malformations
• Abdominal distension
• Irritable/restless, refusal to feed, decreased activity
NON-URGENT
• Mild jaundice
• Transitional stools
• Minor birth trauma
• Minor malformations
• Superficial infections
• Breastfeeding difficulty
• Regurgitation
TREAT IMMEDIATELY
• Maintain TABC
• Respiratory support: Nasal prong O₂ if SpO₂ <91%
(titrate to 91–95%); nasal CPAP if respiratory distress;
intubate if gasping or poor effort.
• Check blood glucose; send CBC and electrolytes
• If sepsis is likely, draw blood culture and give the first
antibiotic dose without delay
• Start IV fluids as per weight and postnatal age
• Manage shock, dehydration and seizures
ASSESS PROMPTLY AND ACT RAPIDLY
• Maintain TABC
• Nasal prong oxygen if SpO₂ <91%
• Check blood glucose and act accordingly
• Start IV fluids (if abdominal distension/GI malformation)
or gavage feeds
• Assess the risk factors for sepsis
• Investigations as per clinical findings
ASSESS AND COUNSEL
• Treat as per indication
• Counsel for breastfeeding
• Explain danger signs and
follow-up
Follow specific STWs
